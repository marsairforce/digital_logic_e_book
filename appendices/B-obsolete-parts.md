# Appendix B — Obsolete and hard-to-find parts

Status: skeleton
Source material:
[notes/74xx_logic.md](../notes/74xx_logic.md) (obsolete section)

## Purpose

Help a reader recognise parts they will meet in old equipment, old
stock drawers and old schematics — and know what to substitute, or why
there is nothing to substitute.

## Framing to write

"What counts as obsolete is complicated, and depends on the logic family
too." That sentence from the notes becomes a short introduction here:

- A function number can be dead in one family and alive in another
- "Still manufactured", "still stocked", "still affordable" and "still
  sensible to design in" are four different things
- Parts the author holds in old stock are listed for datasheet reference,
  clearly separated from design-in recommendations

## Contents to build

- The notes' obsolete table, cleaned and grouped: open-collector gates
  and buffers, AND-OR-INVERT and strobed gates, Nixie drivers (7441/74141),
  early flip-flops and latches, the 7489 64-bit RAM, the 74181 ALU
- For each group: what it was for, why it vanished, the modern substitute
  if one exists
